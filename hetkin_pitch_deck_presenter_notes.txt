HETKIN PITCH DECK — PRESENTER NOTES

01. Hetkin

02. Meet Hendrik. He runs production at a mid-sized greenhouse in Finland, supplying fresh herbs and salad crops to retailers year-round. Lights, irrigation, ventilation, and climate control run around the clock to keep crops healthy and deliveries on schedule. Last winter, the bill got harder to predict — price spikes turned electricity into one of the greenhouse's largest costs, right alongside labour, growing materials, and heating.

03. Hendrik doesn't have an electricity-use problem. He has a timing problem. Every day, the greenhouse uses over 8,000 kilowatt-hours to keep crops healthy and deliveries on track — but some of that automated work is happening during the most expensive hours, without anyone seeing it.

04. Hendrik's own meter data and his own production schedule already contain the answer to when the lights and climate systems should run to keep the same crops healthy for less. He just has no easy place to see it — price, meter, and schedule live in separate places and rarely meet in one decision.

05. Not everything in a greenhouse can move — crop biology and delivery windows won't bend. But supplemental lighting and climate control cycling often can, within limits Hendrik already knows. Where that flexibility exists, it's worth real money. The opportunity isn't to grow less. It's to find the piece of the day that can happen at a better hour.

06. Hetkin takes Hendrik's actual electricity use, his production schedule, and tomorrow's real prices into one suggestion — not another chart to interpret, a specific move to consider: move these automated lighting and climate-control runs to lower-cost hours, keep harvesting and packing unchanged.

07. This runs end to end today: upload meter data, import the schedule, receive a price-aware recommendation, approve or reject it, get a savings report. The next evidence we need isn't whether we can build it — it's how real growers and factories use it.

08. We report both numbers on purpose. For a business like Hendrik's, with genuinely flexible loads, the modeled saving is 15.3% in volatile winter conditions. For a flat, continuous consumer, it's close to zero — and we say that plainly. That precision is what makes the 15.3% figure credible instead of a marketing number. Field validation is the next step.

09. Hendrik stays in control of his greenhouse, not us. Hetkin recommends, shows the reasoning, and he decides yes or no — for Hendrik, it's not about becoming an energy trader, it's a practical answer he can review with his team. Results are checked afterward. No remote automation, no remote control.

10. Hendrik isn't unusual — Finnish day-ahead prices swing significantly within a single day, and Finland had the highest share of negative-price hours anywhere in Europe in 2024. Forward prices for the first quarter of 2026 are already heading clearly above the prior quarter. Our first customer profile — energy-intensive, some flexible load, market-priced electricity — is one we estimate at 1,200 to 1,500 Finnish SMEs nationally; that's our sizing, not a published figure, and it's one of the things we're testing.

11. The market has tools for seeing prices and tools for monitoring usage. Nobody occupies the layer in between: a specific move, made safely, with a verified financial result.

12. A predictable subscription funds the base product. The savings share only applies after we've verified the result — so we only make more when Hendrik does.

13. We've cold-called and emailed about 30 companies matching Hendrik's profile. No pilot signed yet — we're asking for two things: introductions to production leaders with flexible loads, and an energy-market partner with real industrial relationships.

14. Right now, somewhere in Finland, a greenhouse like Hendrik's is running its lights and climate systems through an expensive hour it never had to. Hetkin helps him make the better timing decision — before that hour shows up as a cost on the bill.
